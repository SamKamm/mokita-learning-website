Case Studies

What changes when people talk honestly.

A few examples of our work. All organisations are anonymised unless otherwise agreed.

NHS Trust — Leadership Development

When a senior team stopped pretending everything was fine

A senior leadership team at an NHS trust had been through significant organisational change. On paper, things were functioning. Beneath the surface, trust had broken down and key issues were being avoided in meetings that should have addressed them directly.

What we did: A six-month programme combining whole-team workshops with individual coaching for each director. We started by creating conditions where people could say what they actually thought — and worked from there.

What changed: By the end of the programme, the team reported significantly higher levels of trust and were actively surfacing problems earlier rather than hoping they'd resolve themselves. Two directors described it as the most useful professional development they'd ever experienced.

Youth Charity — Team Culture

Naming the thing that was making good people leave

A mid-sized youth charity had a retention problem. Staff turnover was high, exit interviews were vague, and leadership couldn't identify a clear cause. Morale was low but the reasons felt elusive.

What we did: We ran a listening process across the organisation — small group conversations designed to surface what people weren't saying in formal settings. What emerged was a clear pattern: staff felt their expertise wasn't trusted and their voices didn't reach decisions.

What changed: With that named clearly, the organisation redesigned how decisions were made and introduced a structured feedback process. Staff turnover dropped noticeably in the following year. More importantly, people reported feeling like they actually mattered.

Private Business — Manager Development

Helping a growing business grow up

A fast-growing business had promoted several high-performing individuals into management roles. They were technically brilliant but had received no development as managers. The result was inconsistent leadership, frustrated teams, and a culture that varied wildly between departments.

What we did: A four-month learning programme for the management cohort — monthly workshops on core leadership skills combined with peer coaching between sessions. We focused heavily on self-awareness and the shift from doing to leading.

What changed: Managers reported feeling more confident and less reactive. Teams noticed. The business now has a consistent approach to internal promotion that includes a development component from day one.
