A simple tool for making conflict a bit less rubbish

On the instinct to win or self-punish when disagreements arise — and one reframe that changes everything.

My instinctive response to conflict with a colleague tends to go one of two ways. Either I get defensive and come out swinging — determined to make my case, win the argument, and walk away with my ego intact. Or I go the other way entirely: internalise everything, assume I'm the problem, and spend the rest of the day convincing myself I'm useless.

Both responses share the same underlying logic: that conflict is a winner-takes-all situation, where winning protects the ego and losing destroys it.

But conflict doesn't have to work that way. A different framing: conflict is the meeting of two different perspectives striving for the same goal — whether that's maintaining a relationship, moving an organisation forward, or just getting the bloody project finished.

When people redirect their focus toward that larger shared purpose, something shifts. Neurologically, it moves us away from reactive, threat-based responses — the amygdala doing its thing — toward more considered thinking. Understanding becomes possible. Shutdown becomes less likely.

"We're on the same team."

It sounds almost too simple. And it won't stop the defensive reaction from firing — that's not really in our control. But it recontextualises it. It puts the disagreement inside a bigger frame: one where we're not opponents, we're collaborators who happen to see something differently.

In practice, this shifts things in three ways. It makes conflict more constructive — you're working with someone's anxiety rather than against it. It opens up the possibility of genuine learning — if we're on the same team, their perspective might actually contain something useful. And it can, oddly enough, deepen the connection between people — nothing builds trust quite like a difficult conversation handled well.

Next time you feel a conflict response firing, try it. We're on the same team. Not a magic fix, but a better place to start.
