Why I started this

I spent years managing teams across youth charities, mental health services, education settings, and the NHS. In that time I sat through a lot of training and development. Most of it was forgettable. Some of it was actively unhelpful — generic frameworks dropped onto people whose actual problems were much more human, much more specific, and much harder to package.

The sessions that did make a difference had something in common. They created genuine space for people to think and speak honestly. They didn't pretend complexity away. They took people seriously — including the awkward, unresolved, contradictory parts of their working lives.

That's what I set out to build with Mokita Learning.

How I work

I'm not a management consultant. I don't believe most workplace problems are solved by better processes or smarter strategy. I believe they're solved by people — when those people have the conditions, skills, and courage to be honest with each other.

My background is in psychology, mental health, and frontline leadership rather than corporate consulting. That shapes everything about how I work: I'm interested in what's actually going on for people, not in performing expertise. I ask a lot of questions before I propose anything, and I'd rather tell you we're not the right fit than sell you something that won't help.

Who I work with

I work with a range of organisations — SMEs, charities, NHS trusts, local authorities, and private sector businesses. What they have in common isn't their size or sector. It's that they care about how their people experience work, and they're willing to look honestly at what's getting in the way.

If that sounds like you, I'd love to hear from you.
